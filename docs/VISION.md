# Where Hosted goes from here

The closing argument for the deck. The prototype proves one loop: a person with a phone and no bank account speaks a listing into existence, a traveller books it, and money lands on that phone. Everything below is what that loop unlocks once it exists.

## The closing slide, in five lines

1. **Every host is a business that was never counted.** Hosted gives them a ledger, a reputation and a payout history. That is the raw material of a loan, a lease and a licence.
2. **Trust travels with the person, not the platform.** Community-vouched identity is portable. A host verified in Langa is verified in Soweto.
3. **The channel is the moat.** WhatsApp, voice and cash-send reach the 60 percent of South Africans that every app-store marketplace structurally excludes.
4. **Demand data is a public good.** Where travellers want to go, and what nobody is offering there yet, is exactly what a tourism office or a municipality cannot see today.
5. **It is a pattern, not a product.** Informal-economy tourism looks the same in Windhoek, Maputo and Nairobi. The exclusion is identical, so the fix is too.

## Why it compounds

Each host who gets paid tells the people around them. Each community that verifies its own hosts lowers the cost of trust for the next one. Each booking writes a line of earnings history that did not exist before. The marketplace gets cheaper to grow the more of the informal economy it formalises, because the formalising is the growth.

## Growth and impact opportunities

Each one names the lever, who it reaches, and the impact it moves. They are ordered by how directly they follow from what is already built.

### 1. Earnings history as a financial identity
**Lever.** Every completed booking already writes `hostReceivesCents` to a `Payout`. Render that as a monthly statement a host can download, then offer it to a lender.
**Reach.** Unbanked and underbanked hosts, the majority.
**Impact.** Financial inclusion. A cook with twelve months of Hosted statements has a credit history for the first time. This is the single highest-leverage thing on the list because it turns the platform's exhaust into the host's asset.

### 2. Community Champions as paid local infrastructure
**Lever.** The Champion role in the PRD becomes a paid one: a per-onboarding fee, a share of first bookings, and the endorsement power that already exists in the `COMMUNITY` verification tier.
**Reach.** One person per area with standing and a phone, typically already an informal organiser.
**Impact.** Employment, and the acquisition problem solved from the inside. Supply is sourced by people who know who is good, not by a marketing budget.

### 3. Voice, language and the story as the product
**Lever.** The host's voice note is already captured. Publish it. Let travellers hear the host in isiXhosa with a translation beneath, and let hosts record checkpoint audio for self-guided walks in their own language.
**Reach.** Hosts more comfortable speaking than typing; travellers who came for something real.
**Impact.** Cultural. Stories stop being flattened into English listing copy. The eleven official languages become a feature rather than a barrier, and the platform records culture in the voice of the people who carry it.

### 4. Cooperative supply
**Lever.** Let several hosts publish one listing together, split the payout by rule, and share equipment: one set of bicycles, one bakkie, one kitchen. The stokvel is the organising model South Africans already trust.
**Reach.** Hosts who cannot afford the asset alone.
**Impact.** Lower barrier to entry, higher capacity per town, and a natural path to formalising as a co-op.

### 5. Formalising informal transport
**Lever.** Transport is already a category, gated behind Tier 2 credentials. Pair with a PrDP and roadworthy check flow and the platform becomes the on-ramp for a minibus or bakkie owner to sell scheduled transfers legally.
**Reach.** The largest informal employer in the country.
**Impact.** Safety for travellers, legitimacy and steady demand for drivers, and a route into insurance that the informal sector cannot currently buy.

### 6. Demand data for the towns
**Lever.** The marketplace knows where travellers search and what they cannot find. Publish anonymised demand-by-place to municipal tourism offices and community organisations, with a "nobody offers this here yet" signal.
**Reach.** Local government, NGOs, the 40 percent of towns with no listing on any incumbent platform.
**Impact.** Targeted supply development. A tourism office that knows fifty people searched for a Hogsback hike last month can go find the hiker.

### 7. Domestic and institutional demand
**Lever.** Schools, universities and corporates book local experiences for groups. A group checkout already exists in the trip model.
**Reach.** Domestic travellers, who outnumber international visitors and travel year-round.
**Impact.** Smooths the seasonality that kills informal tourism income, and puts money into the community during the off-season.

### 8. Youth and women first
**Lever.** Seed the marketplace deliberately: student guides in university towns, women-led food and craft. Feature them. Track it.
**Reach.** The two groups with the highest unemployment and the least access to platforms.
**Impact.** The unemployment story told with names and numbers, not a slide.

### 9. Green premium
**Lever.** `SustainabilityTag` exists on every offering. Reward it with placement and a visible badge, and let travellers filter for it.
**Reach.** Hosts using bicycles, recycled materials, local produce, low-impact routes.
**Impact.** An incentive that pays hosts to be greener, aligned with what a growing share of travellers is already choosing.

### 10. Insurance and protection per booking
**Lever.** A platform-level public liability policy priced per booking, and a small traveller-paid safety add-on. Both are line items on a checkout that already exists.
**Reach.** Every host, none of whom can buy cover today; every traveller weighing whether to book a stranger.
**Impact.** Trust made concrete. This is the thing that lets a first-time host compete with an established operator.

### 11. Deep rural reach
**Lever.** The PWA is already offline-first by design. Add a USSD entry point for feature phones and a Champion-assisted flow for hosts with no smartphone at all.
**Reach.** The part of the country with the most to offer and the least connectivity.
**Impact.** Tech inclusion at the very bottom of the ladder, where a single booking a month changes a household.

### 12. Trust as a product
**Lever.** Community-vouched identity, verification tiers and two-way reviews become an API. A rental platform, a lender or a municipality can ask "is this a trusted local host?" and get an answer.
**Reach.** Every host, whose reputation stops being locked inside one app.
**Impact.** Portable dignity. The reputation a host earns here is theirs to carry.

### 13. Savings and group pay
**Lever.** The travel fund in the PRD: friends contribute over months into an escrow for a trip. Extend to hosts saving toward equipment, with the platform as the trusted holder.
**Reach.** Travellers who cannot pay upfront; hosts who cannot save alone.
**Impact.** Financial inclusion on both sides of the marketplace, using the stokvel logic people already trust.

### 14. Regional replication
**Lever.** Nothing in the stack is South Africa-specific except the ID checksum and the payout rails. Namibia, Botswana, Mozambique, Zimbabwe and Kenya have the same informal tourism economy and the same exclusion from the same incumbents.
**Reach.** A region of two hundred million people.
**Impact.** Scale, and the argument that this is a category rather than an app.

## What we will measure

The PRD already commits to these. Say them out loud.

- Hosts onboarded who are unbanked, first-time sellers, or from areas with no incumbent listing
- Rand paid out, and the share via non-bank channels
- Time from registration to first live listing
- Hosts for whom Hosted is a meaningful monthly supplement
- Bookings completed, repeat bookings, and reviews both ways

## What must be true

- **Trust holds.** One safety incident undoes a year of growth. Verification expiry, insurance and a support path come before scale.
- **The host stays the hero.** When host simplicity and traveller convenience conflict, the host wins. The moment that flips, the platform becomes the incumbents it set out to replace.
- **Fees stay visible and low.** Every host sees what they will receive before they publish. An extractive platform is a short-lived one.

## The line to end on

> We built for the host's journey, so the traveller's journey has someone real at the end of it. Scale that, and tourism starts paying the people who make a place worth visiting.
